## What it does
The ElevenLabs voice generator turns written text into spoken audio and lets users create custom voices. Model choice drives the trade-off between expressiveness, language coverage, and per-request length: the Eleven v3 model became generally available on 2026-02-02, supports 70+ languages, and caps each request at 5,000 characters, while Flash v2.5 covers 32 languages with a 40,000-character request limit for low-latency use.

## Pricing
Generation draws from a monthly credit pool. The Free plan costs $0 with 10,000 credits per month; paid entry points, checked 2026-07-30, are Starter at $6 per month (30,000 credits) and Creator at $22 per month (121,000 credits). Instant voice cloning unlocks at Starter, and professional voice cloning at Creator.

## Limits that bite
Per-request character limits vary by model — 5,000 characters on Eleven v3 versus 40,000 on Flash v2.5 — so long scripts on the expressive model must be split. Free output is non-commercial and requires attribution. The Free plan allows three custom voice slots and three concurrent requests.

## How it compares
OpenAI's text-to-speech provides a small fixed voice set and no cloning. Murf emphasizes an editor for narration projects. WellSaid Labs focuses on a curated set of licensed corporate voices.

## Good fit / poor fit
A good fit for producing multilingual, expressive voiceovers and cloned voices from text. A poor fit for uncredited free commercial use, or for generating very long single passages on the expressive v3 model without chunking.
